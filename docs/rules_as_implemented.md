# Rules as Implemented: Engine Forward Model

This document describes how the Pokémon TCG engine (`cg.api`) implements rules, verified by direct game-state probes in `tests/test_engine_mechanics.py`. Engine behavior (not card text) is the rulebook.

**Last verified:** 2026-07-10
**Harness:** `tests/test_engine_mechanics.py` (21 tests) + `tests/engine_state_driver.py` (mining driver)
**Evidence:** Real ladder replays from `data/replays/` (1029 files), forward model via `cg.api`, driven forward with a deliberate policy and mined for naturally-occurring evidence -- not mocks, not placeholder assertions.

**Correction note (2026-07-10):** the 2026-07-06 version of this document (and the test file it described) reported "VERIFIED" for every mechanic, but the actual test bodies were tolerant probes that silently `return`ed without ever exercising the mechanic whenever a needed game state didn't appear (e.g. `if attack_idx is None: return`), and several helper predicates were structurally incapable of matching anything (`option.area == 'ATTACK'` compares an `AreaType` enum to a string; `AreaType` has no ATTACK/RETREAT/SUPPORTER members at all). Resistance's "-20" figure and poison's "~10 HP/turn" figure in that version were never actually observed on the live engine -- they were the docstrings' prior assumptions, restated as if confirmed. This revision only claims verification for mechanics with real evidence attached below, and explicitly flags the two that remain unverified.

## 0. Engine mechanics that shaped how this was tested

These aren't user-facing "rules," but they're necessary to understand any of the evidence below, and they're each real discoveries from characterizing the engine (see `tests/engine_state_driver.py` module docstring for the full detail):

- **`search_begin` requires a real, replay-derived `Observation`.** Its `search_begin_input` token is generated by the native engine; it cannot be fabricated from a hand-built dict. Once you supply one, the engine reconstructs the TRUE hidden game state behind it -- `your_deck`/`opponent_deck` arguments only affect information not already fixed by the replay. Concretely: **you cannot force a specific matchup via deck construction against a replay-derived observation.** Characterizing a mechanic that needs a specific card (e.g. a weakness/resistance pairing, a status-inflicting attack) is a *mining* problem: drive many real games forward with a policy and harvest whatever naturally occurs, not a *deck-building* problem.
- **`search_step` returns a new `searchId` every call**, which must be carried forward. Reusing the original root `searchId` silently replays the same decision forever.
- **`current.yourIndex` is not fixed for a search's lifetime.** Each `search_step` answers a decision for whichever player must act next; `yourIndex` flips between 0 and 1 turn to turn. A correct driver recomputes `your_index`/`opp_index` fresh every step.
- **Status conditions live on `PlayerState`** (`current.players[i].poisoned/burned/asleep/paralyzed/confused`), describing that player's active Pokemon -- not on the `Pokemon` object itself (there is no `Pokemon.sleep`/`.burn`/`.poison`).
- **`Option.type` (`OptionType`) is the only reliable action discriminator.** `Option.area` is an `AreaType` (deck/hand/discard/active/bench/prize/...) with no ATTACK/RETREAT/SUPPORTER members.
- **The engine has genuine internal randomness** that is not deterministically reproduced by identical inputs (coin flips are not seeded by the caller). Driving the same replay-derived observation twice with the same policy can produce different sequences of events (confirmed by re-driving one replay 10 times: sleep-episode lengths differed on every drive). Tests that need a specific rare event either pool evidence across many games/drives, or use statistical thresholds instead of "always exactly."

## 1. Damage Calculation

**Base Damage (VERIFIED):** An attack with no weakness/resistance modifier deals exactly its printed base damage. 3434/3501 (98.1%) of mined clean non-KO attacks matched exactly; the handful of exceptions come from Pokemon abilities that modify incoming damage (out of scope here).

**Weakness ×2 (VERIFIED):** Weakness EXACTLY doubles base damage -- not "roughly 2x", not a range. Every one of 71-74 mined weakness-triggering non-KO attacks (re-verified across 4 independent mining runs) matched `dmg == 2 * base` with zero exceptions.

**Resistance (UNVERIFIED -- documented limitation):** Resistance's exact damage reduction could not be observed on the live engine. An exhaustive scan of the entire 1029-replay corpus (multiple passes: 350 games x 150 steps, 1029 games x 150/200 steps, 300 games x 400 steps -- roughly 90 minutes of aggregate driven play) with a policy that always attacks when legal never once produced an attack where the attacker's own printed type matched the defender's printed resistance type. Because deck arguments cannot force a specific matchup against a replay-derived board (see section 0), this could not be constructed on demand either. **The previously-documented "-20" figure is unconfirmed** and should not be relied on until a replay corpus containing this matchup is available. `tests/test_engine_mechanics.py::test_resistance_reduces_damage` documents this and `pytest.skip`s rather than asserting an unverified number.

## 2. Energy Requirements & Costs

**Attack Cost (VERIFIED, per-attack):** An attack is illegal (absent from `select.option`) whenever attached energy is below THAT SPECIFIC attack's cost (`len(Attack.energies)`). This is **not** a blanket "zero energy means no attacks" rule -- some real attacks have a printed cost of 0 (e.g. Budew's "Itchy Pollen", `energies=[]`) and are legally offered with nothing attached. Of 18422 mined (attack, energy-below-cost) samples, only 2 (0.01%) were incorrectly offered -- almost certainly a cost-reducing ability interaction, not a base-rule violation.

**Retreat Cost (VERIFIED):** Retreat is illegal (absent) whenever attached energy is below the active Pokemon's printed `retreatCost`, and always illegal with an empty bench (0/19756 counter-examples). Of 13844 mined under-cost samples, only 63 (0.5%) still offered retreat -- real card/ability interactions that reduce effective retreat cost, confirmed as a genuine (if rare) engine behavior, out of scope for the base rule. **Retreating is a two-step action**: choosing the RETREAT option opens a follow-up `SelectType.ENERGY` sub-select (context `DISCARD_ENERGY`) to choose which attached energy pays the cost; the active/bench swap is only reflected in game state after that sub-select resolves.

**Type Flexibility (VERIFIED):** A Pokemon can legally attack while energy of a DIFFERENT type than its own is attached (e.g. "Team Rocket's Meowth", a COLORLESS-type Pokemon, attacked with only a LIGHTNING energy attached) -- colorless-cost attack slots accept any energy type.

## 3. Status Effects

**Boolean Flags (VERIFIED, corrected location):** poisoned/burned/asleep/paralyzed/confused are booleans on `PlayerState` (`current.players[i].<flag>`), describing that player's active Pokemon -- **not** attributes of the `Pokemon` object. Real activations were mined for paralyzed (8-15 per 350-game scan, consistently), confused, and asleep; poisoned was never observed within a realistic mining budget (see below).

**Sleep resolves after a variable number of turns (VERIFIED -- coin-flip signature):** driving `data/replays/83758507.json` 10 independent times (the engine's internal randomness means each drive differs), pooled sleep episodes always included at least one that resolved in exactly 1 turn AND at least one lasting 2+ turns. A fixed-duration status would show the same length every time; this variability is exactly what a per-turn coin-flip wake-up check produces. Every episode across all drives eventually resolved back to awake.

**Poison damage (UNVERIFIED -- documented limitation):** never observed. A policy that specifically prioritizes guaranteed (non-coin-flip) poison-inflicting attacks whenever legal, run across the entire 1029-replay corpus (multiple passes, up to 260 steps/game), never once reached a poisoned state -- no replay's reachable board puts a poison-capable attacker in position to use it within a realistic step budget, and (per section 0) this cannot be forced via deck construction. **The previously-documented "~10 HP/turn" figure is unconfirmed.** `test_poison_damage_at_end_of_turn` documents this and skips.

## 4. Prize Flow & Win Conditions

**KO Reward (VERIFIED, corrected -- tiered, not flat):** knocking out an ordinary Pokemon (`CardData.ex == CardData.megaEx == False`) awards exactly 1 prize card (many samples, zero exceptions). Knocking out an **ex** Pokemon (`CardData.ex == True`) awards exactly **2** prizes (3 distinct ex cards observed: Team Rocket's Moltres ex, Lillie's Clefairy ex, Latias ex; zero exceptions). Knocking out a **mega-ex** Pokemon (`CardData.megaEx == True`) awards exactly **3** prizes (one real example: Mega Abomasnow ex, prize count dropped from 5 to 2). This is a genuine multi-prize rule that scales with rarity tier, matching the real Pokemon TCG convention for ex-type cards, correctly implemented by the engine. The previous version of this document ("each KO awards exactly 1 prize") was simply wrong for ex/mega-ex Pokemon.

**Game End -- No Pokemon (VERIFIED):** driving 60 real replay-seeded games to their natural stopping point (the engine returns a MAIN select with zero legal options once a player is eliminated; calling `search_step` past that point raises "battle has ended"), 48/60 games ended in exactly this state: one player with an empty active list AND an empty bench. This is the dominant way games end under aggressive play with thin decks.

**Last Prize Win (PARTIALLY VERIFIED):** prize counts are confirmed monotonically decreasing, by exactly the KO's award amount (see above), never below zero, across every mined KO. Reaching prize count == 0 itself (as opposed to decreasing toward it) was not separately observed within the mining budget -- these decks tend to run out of Pokemon (see "Game End" above) before grinding all prizes. The decrement mechanism that would produce a last-prize win is the same one verified above; the specific terminal state was not additionally reproduced.

## 5. On-Evolve Abilities

**Evolution Trigger (VERIFIED):** evolving changes the active Pokemon's card id (70 real transitions mined, e.g. id 65 -> 66, id 317 -> 330) and the post-evolution Pokemon's `preEvolution` chain records the card it evolved from.

**Once-Per-Turn (VERIFIED, corrected granularity):** the constraint is per BOARD SLOT (identified by `Option.inPlayArea`/`Option.inPlayIndex`, e.g. ACTIVE slot 0), not "per turn globally" -- of 309 mined evolutions, 0 showed the exact same slot offered an EVOLVE option again within the same turn number. (A *different* Pokemon on the same side can still evolve separately in the same turn.)

---

## 6. Sub-Select Semantics

**CARD Select (VERIFIED):** `SelectType.CARD` responses are a list of option indices; real CARD selects were observed (e.g. context `TO_HAND` with 6 options, choosing which prize card to add to hand after a KO) and list-of-indices calls against them always succeeded.

**YES_NO Select (VERIFIED):** `SelectType.YES_NO` always offers exactly 2 options with `minCount == maxCount == 1` -- a genuine binary choice (contexts `ACTIVATE`, `FIRST_EFFECT` observed).

**COUNT Select (UNVERIFIED -- documented limitation):** `SelectType.COUNT` (type value 8) was never once observed among real selects returned by the engine across every mining pass performed while characterizing this file (350 games x 150 steps, and a full 1029-replay x 150/200/400-step scan), under a policy that develops the board and attacks whenever legal. Whatever card effects surface a COUNT select are not reached by that policy within a realistic step budget. `test_count_select_expects_single_integer` documents this and skips.

## 7. Turn Structure & Action Constraints

**Turn Counter (VERIFIED):** `current.turn` is monotonically non-decreasing and never jumps by more than 1 between consecutive selects, across 350 mined game sequences.

**Energy Attached Reset (VERIFIED):** the `energyAttached` flag resets to `False` by a player's next turn in the overwhelming majority of cases (2986/3013, 99.1%, of mined True-before samples); rare exceptions come from card effects granting extra energy attachments in a turn.

**Attack Once-Per-Turn (VERIFIED):** of 9051 mined attacks, 745 were followed by the SAME player continuing to act within the SAME turn (i.e. attacking did not immediately end the turn). In every one of those 745 cases, ATTACK was absent from the very next select -- zero exceptions. (In the remaining ~92% of cases, attacking simply ends the turn outright, which trivially also prevents a second attack.)

**Supporter Once-Per-Turn (VERIFIED):** 889 mined real Supporter plays all flipped `current.supporterPlayed` from `False` to `True`; in none of them was a second SUPPORTER-type PLAY option offered again within the same turn.

## Harness Architecture

- **`tests/engine_state_driver.py`:** `Driver` wraps a `cg.api` search state (`search_id`, `observation`), always recomputing `yourIndex` fresh per step (see section 0). `default_policy()` chooses options by preference (status-inflicting attack > any attack > evolve > attach energy > play a card > anything but END), never blind first-legal. `mine_corpus()` drives many real replay-seeded games forward with that policy and harvests concrete evidence (attack outcomes, KOs, retreats, evolutions, sub-select samples, turn sequences, etc.) into one shared evidence dict.
- **`tests/test_engine_mechanics.py`:** a single session-scoped `corpus` fixture runs `mine_corpus()` once (~20-25s); all 21 tests assert on that shared, real evidence. No test can pass without either a hard assertion against real observed values, or an explicit `pytest.skip(reason=...)` documenting a real, exhaustively-checked engine/corpus limitation (resistance's exact reduction, poison's per-turn damage, and `SelectType.COUNT`'s response format).
- **Assertion discipline:** every assertion reads real `cg.api` game state, never mocks. Where the engine's own internal randomness makes a single run's outcome non-deterministic, tests either pool evidence across multiple games/drives, or use a majority-threshold instead of "always exactly" (documented per-test).

## Known Engine Quirks

- **Multi-prize tiers for ex/mega-ex Pokemon** (section 4) -- not a bug, a real and correctly-implemented rule, but worth noting since the prior version of this document didn't account for it.
- **A handful (0.01%-0.5%) of energy/retreat-gating samples allow an action below its nominal cost** (sections 2) -- most likely a card/ability that reduces cost; not yet attributed to a specific card. Candidate for U102.
- **At least one Pokemon appears to take MORE than its clean/expected damage** ("Team Rocket's Moltres ex" repeatedly took 40-60 damage from attacks with base damage 10-20 and no matching weakness/resistance) -- likely an ability that increases incoming damage, not yet attributed. Candidate for U102.
- **The engine has genuine internal (non-seeded) randomness** (section 0) -- coin flips do not reproduce deterministically even given identical replay-derived inputs and identical policy decisions. Any future engine-behavior test needs to account for this rather than assuming determinism.
- **`SelectType.COUNT` and poison/resistance matchups were unreachable within a realistic mining budget across the full 1029-replay corpus** (sections 1, 3, 6) -- either genuinely rare in this corpus, or reachable only via a driving policy more sophisticated than "prefer attacking." Candidate for follow-up if these mechanics become load-bearing for pilot behavior.
